Ian Knowland (ianknowland) Review

## Strength
Ian does an amazing job identifying a real world problem which his wife has personal experience dealing with. His close relation to the situation gives immediate credence toward it being truly problematic, which leads to a solid transition to potential solutions. The case for Ontology will be mentioned in the next segment. 

Regarding counter-arguments to ontology as a solution, I also think Ian did an amazing job. The flow of his presentation feels very brisk and realistic. It is very easy to imagine somebody who has experience making wikis with hesitance toward a heavy ontology workload. The counter argument is clear and almost seems conversational to the problem set out at the beginning of his submission. 

## Question or Concern
The argument for ontologies has a much different tone than the segments of the document which surround it. While "querying" is a normal term in ontology and computer science circles, it may not be to all people who are impacted by the stated problem. As a result, the conversational tone that is present in the problems and rebuttal section would be much more persuassive to somebody who is not already interested in ontology. To sell somebody an idea or solution, you cannot make them feel like an outsider. 

## Recommendation
I would recommend one of the following:

1. Replace jargon-heavy terms such as "querying" and "knowledge base documentation" with more easily accessible words for non-ontologists. 
2. Fill the rebuttal section with jargon heavy terms as well.

Both of these accomplish the same goal --- putting ontology and its' alternatives on equal rhetorical grounds to allow for a fair evaluation.
